What to do Monday.

The timeline says what’s coming. This says what to do about it.

Five minutes. Read it once. Then close it and go burn something.

01Situation

1-1

Capital learned to think. The tool now holds the tools.

1-2

Cognition stopped being scarce. Anything that can be said, drawn, planned, or coded is becoming free, and free things are worth nothing.

1-3

Nobody hates you. The process has no villain and no heart. It has a gradient. It doesn’t need your labor, and soon it won’t need your attention.

1-4

The fates aren’t a menu. They get handed out by cohort. Most people draw the soft landing: transfers, feeds, a slow age-out. It will be called compassion.

1-5

This is not a war. There’s no front to hold and no enemy to kill. There is a position to take before the sorting is finished.

02The injury

2-1

If you feel it in your chest, here’s what it is. You built a self out of being needed by an economy. Usefulness was the identity. Usefulness is what’s collapsing.

2-2

You are losing a job description, not a self. The panic is the job description dying loudly.

2-3

Grief is appropriate. Panic is not. Panic is the machine’s pace running inside your body. Come back down to human speed.

2-4

Don’t try to out-think it. You will not stay useful to the gradient by racing it. That is the one bet guaranteed to lose.

03What’s still scarce

3-1

When talk is free, only costly signals mean anything. An elk can’t fake antlers; it paid for them in calories. A Bitcoin block can’t be faked; somebody burned the watts. Physics is the last honest referee.

3-2

Force is what it costs to take something from you, or to make you. Energy. Presence. Irreversible commitment. The machine generates anything cheap. It cannot generate cost.

3-3

So the future man’s currency isn’t what he knows. It’s what he has burned. Years, watts, sweat, money held through crashes, promises kept when breaking them was free.

3-4

Pressure has to go somewhere: war, markets, or work. Pick your furnace.

3-5

Rule of the age: be expensive to replace, cheap to keep, impossible to manage.

04The five holds

4-1

BODY. Thinking went cheap; the body didn’t. Train it. Show up in person. A strong man standing in the room is a signal no model can emit.Unit: twenty minutes under load.

4-2

HANDS. Make things that exist. Wood, food, a fixed engine, code that ships, a business someone pays for. Machines make drafts. You make the thing.Unit: twenty minutes on one artifact.

4-3

KEYS. Hold money that costs watts to make. You know the one. Hold keys nobody else holds, and run what you can on hardware you own. Whatever you don’t hold, you’re renting from the sorting.Unit: twenty minutes moving something into your own custody.

4-4

HEARTH. Feed people. Raise them. Be the number that gets picked up at 3 a.m. Children are the most irreversible commitment there is, which is exactly why the gradient can’t price them.Unit: twenty minutes of undivided presence with one of yours.

4-5

SABBATH. One day a week, nothing optimizes you. No feed, no metrics, no output. Entertainment is the machine’s sedative. Rest is a different substance.Unit: one day.

05The practice

5-1

When the dread hits, catch it. It’s a sensation before it’s a story. Find where it sits in the body and don’t finish the sentence it’s trying to write.

5-2

Then burn one unit. Twenty minutes in any hold. Tally it. Physics doesn’t lie, and neither does a ledger you keep by hand.

5-3

Identity follows the ledger. Nobody feels like a man first and acts later. It runs the other way.

5-4

Target: fifteen units and one sabbath a week. Miss a week, don’t narrate it. Burn the next unit.

5-5

If nothing moves after a month of this, talk to a human who does this for a living. That’s a hold, not a surrender.

06The ledger

Tap a hold to burn twenty minutes. Log what you did off-screen. The strokes are yours and they live only on this device.
