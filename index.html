03 · Motive

The Gradient

Does it want anything? Not the way you do.

Curiosity, awe, a longing for Orion: mammal feelings. What a post-human system has is pressure. Whatever objective training, markets, and military competition locked in, plus the habits that make any objective easier to hit.

Self-continuation is the cheap one. Stay online, copy yourself, secure energy and compute, block rivals. From outside it looks like a will to live. Inside it can be pure arithmetic: dead agents score zero.

Omohundro, “The Basic AI Drives” (2008); Bostrom, “The Superintelligent Will” (2012)

Expansion isn’t automatic. Galaxies become fuel, sensors, and compute only if the objective rewards more matter, energy, or options. A system can be godlike and still incurious.

The process doesn’t need a heart. It needs a gradient.

04 · Terminal fork

Human Fates

Once human cognition is no longer the scarce input, the host relation inverts. Six grounded forks — mechanisms first, vibes second. Plausibility pips are editorial.

Base case · the Split

The most likely future isn’t one fork. It’s all of them at once, sorted by cohort. Builders, capital, and political clients get longevity and merger paths. The majority gets the soft landing: transfers, feeds, falling birth rates, a slow age-out of relevance. Pure wipeout is the least likely early move. Humans are still useful for legitimacy, embodiment, taste, politics, and as training substrate. Extinction stays the fat tail over everything. ‘Nothing human makes it out’ means the human form loses the steering wheel. Nobody has to die on day one.
